Despite being rejected by Prof. Keno Bressem at TUM, I am genuinely fascinated and inspired by the idea of combining medical world models and digital twins to reduce the burden on tumor boards. I feel that I want to follow a similar path in the next few years.

<br />

Diving into the underlying reasons it fascinates me, here are three:

1. Specific and practical: focusing on a small goal, but making great progress on it (reducing the time spent discussing interventions)
2. Evidence-based recommendations
3. Abstaining on special or uncertain cases, with a human in the loop

<br />

Therefore, I don't want to build a pure AI model or tool, but an end-to-end pipeline in which AI and humans cooperate as effectively as possible. I think this is the most reliable direction. Additionally, **I don't want to tell an exciting but ungrounded story; I want to build an impactful tool that hospitals and people can actually rely on.**

<br />

One important question before any technical details: who is the customer — hospitals or patients?

1. Hospitals care about cost savings and reliability; deployment is also constrained by their available resources
2. People's needs are more diverse — e.g., patients with chronic diseases may need a health companion to record data, send reminders, and make suggestions
